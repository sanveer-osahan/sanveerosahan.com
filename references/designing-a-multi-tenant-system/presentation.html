One Product, Many Workspaces

A B2B product sells one instance of itself to each company. The company gets a private tenant. The people inside it get accounts. One user never sees another company's data.

The same three layers

Both examples use one product, one tenant per company, and many users per tenant. Only the names change.

LayerSlackGoogle Workspace
Product Slack, sold to the company Google Workspace, sold to the company
Tenant A workspace, at acme.slack.com A domain, at acmerobotics.com
User A member account in that workspace A mailbox on that domain
What users share Public channels, private channels, direct messages Mail, calendars, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides
Who pays The company, per member seat The company, per user account
Who controls access The workspace admin The domain admin

The company is the customer

The contract sits with the company, not with the person who logs in. One sale brings many users.

The tenant is the wall

Data, search and permissions stop at the tenant edge. Acme cannot read a Bluefin channel or file.

An admin sits in the middle

The admin adds people, removes them and sets the rules. The vendor does not do this per user.

Company names, people and addresses on this page are examples. They are not real accounts. Slack and Google Workspace are products of their owners; this page only explains the model.
